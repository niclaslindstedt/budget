# Investment sheet

The Investment sheet is where you track what your investments are worth.
It has two tables, because two kinds of investment behave very
differently.

## Holdings

The first table is a broad catalog: index funds, a basket of shares,
gold, silver, crypto, a bond — anything you hold and want to watch grow.
You record its current value by hand whenever you check it, and the sheet
keeps the history so you can chart it later. The purchase amount you paid
shows up as the first value automatically.

What matters most here is **how the holding is wrapped**, because that
decides the tax when you eventually sell:

- **ISK** (Investeringssparkonto) and **KF** (Kapitalförsäkring) are taxed
  a little every year on a schablon, so selling carries no extra
  capital-gains tax — the net value is the full value.
- A regular **depå** is taxed 30 % on the profit when you sell, so its net
  value is lower than its market value.

## Private stocks

The second table is for single stocks you've bought privately, tracked
share by share. Record each buy or sell with its date, the number of
shares, and the price — the sheet works out how many shares you hold and
your average cost per share using the Swedish average method
(genomsnittsmetoden): a buy blends into the average, a sell just lowers
the count. Update the current price per share whenever you like (enter the
price of one share, or a total value and a share count and let the sheet
divide).

Each position is owned either **privately** or **by your company**, which
changes its net value: a private sale is taxed 30 % on the gain, a company
sale 20.6 %. The sheet doesn't care where the money goes when you sell —
only that the shares moved.

## Visualize value

From the sheet's "…" menu, **Visualize value** charts the combined worth
of every holding and stock over time as one line. The buttons below the
graph zoom the window, and **Show net value** swaps the market value for
what the whole portfolio would be worth after tax if you sold today.

Investments also roll into your **net worth** on the Insights sheet, under
their own category.
